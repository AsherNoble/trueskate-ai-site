03HOW IT WORKS

Five stages. Everything between capture and touch is replaceable; the only interface between stages is a frame buffer and a stream of touch events. The system started on CMA-ES — an evolutionary optimizer rewarded by the trick name read off-screen by Apple Vision OCR, which is what landed the 360 flips in section 05 — and is now built around two-stage behavioral cloning: it learns gestures from expert play instead of searching for them.

01demonstrationsexpert True Skate gameplay captured on real iPhones; the only data the policy learns from.
02trace extractora small U-Net reads each frame and finds the finger on the glass, self-labeled from the agent’s own known gestures.
03strokesper-frame touch points fit into discrete curved drags (waypoints, duration, easing, curvature), normalized across three screen sizes.
04policya transformer takes recent frames plus the last few strokes and predicts the next stroke.
05actuationstrokes executed on physical iPhones via Appium + WebDriverAgent; a physical hand is in design.

An earlier CMA-ES baseline and a collapsed PPO + HER attempt taught me what this noisy real-time setting could and couldn’t give back.
