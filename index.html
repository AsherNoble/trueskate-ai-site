TrueSkate-AI — an animated sculpture

A personal research project on learning fine motor control from vision alone: a robot hand playing True Skate on unmodified iPhones, seeing only screen frames and acting only through touch.

Asher Noble
log started 2026-06
status in progress

01WHAT IT IS

I’m training a machine learning model to play True Skate on unmodified physical iPhones. It sees only screen frames and acts only through touch — no access to the game’s code.

The controls are continuous, the feedback is delayed, and the game reports a trick’s name on screen but nothing else.

My conjecture: I can’t assimilate the whole internet in a lifetime, and I can’t train a frontier LLM with <$1000 of compute — but I did master True Skate well within a lifetime, so it may be possible to train a machine learning model to play it on <$1000 of compute.

The long-term vision is a tendon-driven robotic hand tapping and swiping on an actual phone screen (read section 04 for a thorough overview). I am currently experimenting with offline learning with the intention to translate the 2D trajectories inferred by the trained model into 3D trajectories taken by the fingers of the robotic hand.

03HOW IT WORKS

The prototype of this project used covariance matrix adaptation evolution strategy (CMA-ES) – a sample-efficient RL approach – with the on-screen trick name as the reward signal. The project is now being built around two-stage behavioral cloning, outlined below:

DemonstrationsExpert demonstrations of my True Skate gameplay are screen-recorded and saved.
Data collectionA self-supervised pipeline generates and executes random synthetic touches on two iPhones plugged into my homelab (see Fig. 3), and records the resulting gameplay.
Inverse dynamics modelA spatiotemporal CNN is trained on the corpus from step 02 to reconstruct the gestures that produced a given sequence of video frames. It is then used to label the expert demonstrations from step 01.
PolicyA CNN–Transformer is trained through behavioural cloning to predict upcoming gestures from recent gameplay frames and previous gestures.
InferenceThe policy predicts gestures in real time, which are converted into 3D trajectories and executed by the robotic hand using inverse kinematics.

04THE HAND

Most robotic hands in the literature are designed to grip and manipulate objects, which means they are optimised for torque rather than speed. Mine needs relatively little torque but requires very high speed, acceleration and precision. I’m basing my design on the open-source orcahand v2 – with gimbal motors instead of high-torque servos – and intend to make hardware modifications until it is optimised for virtual finger skating.
