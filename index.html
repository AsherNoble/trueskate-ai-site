TrueSkate-AI — an animated sculpture

A personal research project on learning fine motor control from vision alone: a robot hand playing True Skate on unmodified iPhones, seeing only screen frames and acting only through touch.

Asher Noble

status in progress

01WHAT IT IS

I’m training a machine learning model to play True Skate on unmodified physical iPhones. It sees only screen frames and acts only through touch — no access to the game’s code.

The controls are continuous, the feedback is delayed, and the game reports a trick’s name on screen but nothing else.

My conjecture: I can’t assimilate the whole internet in a lifetime, and I can’t train a frontier LLM with <$1000 of compute — but I did master True Skate well within a lifetime, so it may be possible to train a machine learning model to play it on <$1000 of compute.

The long-term vision is a tendon-driven robotic hand tapping and swiping on an actual phone screen (read section 04 for a thorough overview). I am currently experimenting with offline learning with the intention to translate the 2D trajectories inferred by the trained model into 3D trajectories taken by the fingers of the robotic hand.

03HOW IT WORKS

The protoype of this project used covariance matrix adaptation evolution strategy (CMA-ES) – a sample-efficient RL approach – with the on-screen trick name as the reward signal. The project is now being built around two-stage behavioral cloning, outlined below:

01demonstrationsexpert demonstrations of my True Skate gameplay are screen-recorded and saved.
02gesture corpusa script performs random gestures via Appium + WebDriverAgent and collects the video frames they produce.
03trace extractora small U-Net is trained to predict the gestures that produced a given sequence of video frames for the corpus created in step 02.
04policya transformer (or another architecture) is trained to take recent frames plus recent gestures and predict the next few gestures.
05inferencegestures are inferred and translated from 2D gestures to 3D trajectories executed by the robotic hand on the iPhone screen.

An earlier CMA-ES baseline and a collapsed PPO + HER attempt taught me what this noisy real-time setting could and couldn’t give back.

04THE HAND

Most robotic hands in the literature are designed to grip and manipulate objects, which means they are optimised for torque rather than speed. Mine needs relatively little torque but requires very high speed, acceleration and precision. I’m basing my design on the open-source ORCA hand – with gimbal motors instead of high-torque servos – and intend to make hardware modifications until it is optimised for virtual finger skating.
